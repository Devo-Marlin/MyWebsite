Overview

What I wanted to build

The goal for this build isn't outright speed — it's a mechanically simple, reliable NA Miata that rewards good driving inputs and still looks clean enough to show. That means prioritizing weight reduction, suspension geometry, and driver feedback over parts that just look fast.

Constraints are mostly self-imposed: keep the car reliable enough to drive regularly, keep modifications reversible where possible, and document every stage so decisions can be revisited later instead of forgotten.

Engineering / Design

Suspension, weight, and mechanical simplicity

Early work focused on chassis prep — stripping trim and interior for rust repair and weight savings, then addressing surface rust and refinishing the engine bay before anything mechanical went back on. From there, the build moved into coilover installation and suspension mockup to get the car to ride height before corner-balancing and alignment.

The build log below tracks each stage as it happens rather than as a finished write-up — it will keep growing as suspension, wheels/tires, brakes, and interior work progress.

Build Log

Progress, in order

01

Tear Down Begins

Stripped bumpers, fenders, and interior carpet to prep for rust repair and weight savings. A few spots need patching, but the frame is in good shape overall.

02

Chassis Prep & Paint

Cleaned up surface rust, applied rust preventative, and refinished the engine bay in satin black ahead of seam sealer and suspension install.

03

Suspension Mockup

Installed coilovers and test-fit control arms to get the car to track height ahead of corner-balancing, alignment, and wheel/tire selection.

Results / Lessons

What's held up, what hasn't

Worked

Doing rust repair and weight-saving teardown before any suspension work meant fewer surprises once the car was on coilovers.

Learned

Mocking up ride height early made it obvious which wheel/tire combinations would actually clear before money was spent on them.

Next

Corner-balancing, alignment, and wheel/tire finalization, followed by brake and interior work as the build continues.
